The problem

Accounts are scattered, and the paperwork is different everywhere

After a death, families often don't know which retirement accounts, health savings accounts, life insurance policies or crypto holdings exist, or where they are held. Each custodian has its own claim process, its own forms and its own document rules.

  • Scattered custodians. A lifetime of jobs can leave 401(k)s, IRAs and HSAs spread across many firms.
  • Paperwork at every step. Claim forms, signature guarantees, estate papers and follow-ups, one institution at a time.
  • Death certificates. Most custodians want their own certified copy, and families often don't know how many to order.
  • Crypto with no beneficiary option. Some exchanges don't let owners name a beneficiary, so heirs must go through the estate.

What Heirline does

01

Search

Look up a loved one across a network of custodians to see which accounts exist and whether you're a named beneficiary. (Sample network in this prototype.)

02

Document scan

Read tax forms and statements, such as 1099-R, 5498 and 5498-SA, right in your browser to spot accounts the family didn't know about. It recognizes major custodians such as Fidelity, Vanguard, Schwab, Principal, TIAA, Voya, Merrill, E*TRADE, Optum, Kraken and more.

03

Automatic state search

States that publish open data are searched automatically. Today that's California (records of $1,000+, data as of 2026-10-14). The Search all covered states switch, on by default, checks covered states even if they never lived there.

04

Every other state

All 50 states, DC and Puerto Rico have guided manual search: pick states from a searchable list, then get a one-click link to MissingMoney.com and each state's official search, plus a per-state checklist (checked, nothing found, found) that feeds your tracker.

05

Life insurance

Guidance on the free NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator and MIB's paid locator, veterans' coverage, and a checklist of clues like premium payments and employer group policies. The document scan spots insurer names and premium notices, and locator requests go straight into your tracker.

06

Claim plan

Step-by-step instructions and a document checklist for each custodian, a ready-to-edit cover letter to copy or download, and a death-certificate count.

07

Tracker

Follow every claim from not started to paid, with notes and follow-up dates.

08

Find my own money

A second mode for your own forgotten money. Search unclaimed property under your name and any other or maiden names, check the Labor Department's Retirement Savings Lost and Found and PBGC, and get a checklist and request letter for each past employer. It also covers savings bonds, old HSAs and bank accounts, and IRS refunds, and includes a beneficiary checkup covering 66 major providers (401(k) recordkeepers, IRA and brokerage firms, HSAs, crypto platforms and banks) with a searchable picker, each provider's update page or how-to, and an honest note where no beneficiary option exists.

09

Heirline Assistant

A built-in chat with 58 answers on claims, documents, life insurance, probate basics, HSAs, crypto and finding your own lost money. Ask for my results to get a summary of your found accounts and plan. It runs in your browser and the chat isn't saved.

For financial institutions

Fewer stalled claims, better-prepared beneficiaries

We're exploring tools for custodians and plan providers: beneficiary checkups that help account owners keep designations current while they're alive, and complete, well-organized claim packets that make the process smoother for families and staff after a death.

How it protects your privacy

Honest about what this prototype does

  • 🔒 Stays in your browser. Searches, scanned documents, claim plans, the tracker and the assistant chat are processed and saved only in your browser (local storage). Nothing is sent to a Heirline server; there isn't one.
  • No real SSNs. The app only compares the last four digits against fictional sample data, and clears the field after each search. Please don't enter real Social Security numbers.
  • Sample custodian data. The custodian network is made up. Real lookups would require partnerships with each institution.
  • Public records only. The California search uses the State Controller's publicly published unclaimed-property file.
  • It's a prototype. It hasn't had a security audit, and it isn't ready for real estate settlement. Clearing your browser data removes everything it saved.
