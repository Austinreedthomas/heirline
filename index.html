Find life insurance policies

Life insurance often goes unclaimed because no one knows the policy exists. Two locator services and a few clues can help.

NAIC Life Insurance Policy Locator

Free

Run by the National Association of Insurance Commissioners. If you're a beneficiary, executor or legal representative, you submit one request with details from the death certificate: legal name, dates of birth and death, SSN or ITIN, veteran status, and your relationship. Participating life insurers and annuity companies search their records.

  • If a policy is found and you're the beneficiary or authorized to receive information, the insurer contacts you directly.
  • If nothing is found, or you're not the beneficiary, you won't hear back. Not every insurer participates, so silence doesn't prove there's no policy.
  • It can take up to 90 business days. It only works for people who have died.

MIB Policy Locator Service

$75, paid

MIB searches its database of life insurance applications made since 1996 at member insurers. A report naming the insurers and their contact details is mailed within 21 business days. It shows applications, not whether a policy was issued or is still in force, and it doesn't cover most employer group policies. It needs a notarized form, an original death certificate (returned to you) and a $75 money order or certified check, non-refundable.

Veterans and service members

VA life insurance claims use VA Form 29-4125e with the death certificate. VGLI claims use form SGLV 8283, sent to Prudential's Office of Servicemembers' Group Life Insurance (OSGLI). If no beneficiary was named, SGLI and VGLI pay in a set order: spouse, then children, parents, the estate's executor, then other next of kin.

Retirement and lost accounts

DOL Retirement Savings Lost and Found

The U.S. Department of Labor's free database of workplace retirement plans that owe people money. You sign in with an identity-verified Login.gov account and it searches your own SSN, then shows the plan and the administrator to contact. Heirline never sees your SSN.

PBGC unclaimed pensions

The Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation lists people owed pensions from plans that ended. Search by last name and the last 4 digits of your SSN (type them on PBGC's site, not here). A match is a first step: you then contact PBGC.

Other places to look

Savings bonds and Treasury securities

Treasury Hunt closed on September 30, 2025. Matured, unclaimed Treasury securities now go through state unclaimed-property programs, so search every state you've lived in. For lost, stolen or destroyed paper bonds, use TreasuryDirect's forms.

State programs ↗ Bond forms ↗ About the change ↗

Old HSAs

HSAs stay yours after you leave a job. Check old benefits paperwork or pay stubs for the HSA provider's name, then log in or call. Dormant HSAs can be turned over to the state, so the unclaimed-property step also covers them.

Old bank and brokerage accounts

Look for old statements, 1099 tax forms, or emails from banks and brokers. Accounts with no activity for a few years are sent to the state, so search each state you've lived in. Brokerage shares may be sold by the state, but you can usually still claim their value.

IRS refunds

Use Where's My Refund to check a recent return or start a trace for a lost check. If you never filed for a year you were owed a refund, you generally have three years to claim it. For a missing check, the IRS line is 800-829-1954.
